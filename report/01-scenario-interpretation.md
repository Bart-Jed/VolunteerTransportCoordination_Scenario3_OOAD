
## Organisation
- Kerry Community Link coordinates non-emergency journeys for residents who have difficulty using ordinary public transport
- Journeys include community activities, shopping, education, social appointments and essential local services
- Volunteer drivers use their own cars, while partner organisations occasionally provide accessible vehicles
## Present Process
1. A one-way, return or recurring request comes in from passenger/relative/community workers.
2. Drivers provide usual availability, preferred areas, maximum journey distance and any vehicle limitations. 
3. Passengers requests and drivers availability get matched through provisional offers, which drivers can decline without giving a reason
4. Coordinators sometimes allocate a familiar driver before checking whether documentation is current Otherwise a driver that meets the accessibility needs is allocated.
5. If no driver is free, the request can go on a waiting list, to a partner or be turned down.
6. A passenger may cancel, alter a time or change the destination. 
7. Alters in the journey may cause the driver to become unavailable, as well as drivers illnesses, vehicle problems or weather.
8. Any replacement for the journey must meet the original passenger and vehicle requirements.
9. Drivers receive information on journeys and passengers through printed schedules, as not all drivers use email or mobile apps.
10. If a passenger isn't ready or doesn't appear, the driver may decide on waiting time or who to telephone.
11. The passenger receives the final arrangements, though whether this covers the return is unknown.
12. Drivers complete records of collection, arrival, return and any unsuccessful journey.
