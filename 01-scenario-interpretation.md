# What it must include:
- Organisation
	- Kerry Community Link coordinates non-emergency journeys for residents who have difficulty using ordinary public transport
	- Journeys include community activities, shopping, education, social appointments and essential local services
	- Volunteer drivers use their own cars, while partner organisations occasionally provide accessible vehicles
- Present Process
	- Drivers use their own cars, unless provided through partners
	- Completed journeys recorded through notes, email, spreadsheets and weekly schedules (printed).
	- Different coordinators may describe the same destination or passenger need differently, and changes are not always reflected on every copy
- Problems
	1. Vehicle may not suit the journey/passenger, missing accessibility needs?
	2. Printed schedules not dynamic, if one is cancelled the driver must adapt to it, causing delays
	3. Return journey not being confirmed, causing anxiety/stress to the passenger, as well as multiple communication attempts
	4. Passenger may not receive final arrangements for transport if requested by community worker.
	5. Not enough drivers
	6. No way to tell whether a driver declined, was unavailable or was never contacted
- Consequences
	1. Journey wasted as passenger cannot access vehicle, effectively denying another passengers journey
	2. Any changes would stay unknown to the driver if driver is out on the road. Could result in driver frustration, wasted resources and time.
	3. Could lead to the passenger losing interest in the service.
	4. Could lead to passenger confusion, anxiety and stress, as well as driver's time wasted
	5. Some passengers might not have a journey at all, allowing space for overworking other drivers.
	6. Could lead to passenger anxiety/frustration, as well as driver confusion
